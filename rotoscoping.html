01 / Pre-Chopping & Preparing the Video Step 1

I started by taking my full 60-second raw video file and splitting it evenly into twelve smaller clips that are minimum 5 seconds each. This made managing the large video much simpler. To stop my editing platform from lagging, I dropped down the preview playback resolution and temporarily boosted the image contrast so the edges of my subject clearly popped out from the scenery.

Tactical Tips & Tricks

A few shortcuts and production workflows I use to save hours of processing time and keep my cutouts looking sharp.

Boost Your Contrast First

Before you start tracing, try making your video extremely bright and high-contrast using an adjustment effect. Making dark colors darker and light colors lighter helps the computer easily figure out where the subject ends and where the background begins. You can simply turn the effect off when you are done!

Lock Down Your Work

Never forget to click the "Freeze" button at the bottom right corner of your video viewer once your tracking looks perfect. This saves your outlines directly onto your computer's storage drive, preventing the editing software from wasting power recalculating your lines every time you click play.

Chop Long Clips Apart

Don't try to outline a long, continuous video all in one go. Instead, split your video file down into small, manageable pieces of about 2 to 3 seconds each. Working in tiny intervals stops the computer from drifting off course and making mistakes as the video plays forward.
